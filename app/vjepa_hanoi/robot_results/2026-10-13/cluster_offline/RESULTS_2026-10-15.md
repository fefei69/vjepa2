# Categorical planning and fps-1 models: one game move offline (cluster H200, no robot), 2026-10-15

Request (2026-10-15): "make the 1 fps version work, or retrain multiple versions"; "you have 10 hr to test multiple
variants... make frameskip large (1 fps) and do something special for sampling, maybe categorical sampling? don't
sample from too uniform distribution". Fairness to the official recipe is not required for this version. Every change
is still an opt-in flag (defaults unchanged) and is labelled below. Nothing here touched the robot.

**Labels.**
- **B** = paper protocol (`--protocol paper`, 3 goals, CEM 800x10, H=1, the repo's box).
- **P** = planner deviation: per-stage goals `stages`, horizon, `--sampling cat|data`, a model step other than fps 4,
  `--schedule_scale`.
- **A** = task aid: `hold_xy`, `hold_grasp`, `hold_open`, `fix_x`.
- **S** = safety-envelope constraint on the categorical planner: it skips steps the arm would refuse or undo.
  - `--guard_floor_mm 166.13`: no step more than 3 mm sideways with an end below robot_io's low floor (transit z −
    25 mm). robot_io's low-lateral guard aborts the run on such a step.
  - `--box_feasible`: no step ending outside goal_planner's workspace box (the training data's range, padded). The
    server clamps such a step anyway.

  Both use only the arm's workspace numbers. They carry no stage or goal information.

## Summary

1. **Categorical sampling is the fix.**
   - The planner scores a finite set of actions taken from the training data: stay, close, open, and 48 k-means
     centres of the expert's moving steps at the model's step (`--sampling cat`, `mpc_utils.cem_categorical`).
   - Every code is scored at H=1 and H=2. So every planned step is an action shape the world model was trained on.
   - Continuous CEM at fps 1 samples in a 187.5 mm box, and about 98% of its first samples are larger than any
     training step.
2. **fps 1 works with it. The best model is E-1fps**: official init + state token, data.fps 1, trained on all expert
   moves of the six tasks, 25 epochs (best epoch 25, val 0.588).
   - **Open loop** (every row of the six held-out first moves): gripper intent 0.97, lateral error 2.0 mm, vertical
     2.4 mm, direction cosine 1.00. It plans "close" on 18/18 grasp-stage rows, and never at the hover or in the air.
   - **Closed loop on recorded frames, pick** (hover → grasp → 30 mm lift, §3): **6/6** with `cat + stages + H=1` + S.
     No task aid. Every case: −78 mm, −23 mm, close, hold, lift +81 mm.
   - **Pick with the arm's current 36 mm step rule** (`--max_speed 0.036`; the planned step is scaled when executed):
     **6/6**.
   - **Closed loop, whole move** (to the release on the target peg): **4–5/6** in both arm options: 4/6 at full step
     size, 5/6 with the 36 mm cap and the time schedule ×1.5 (`one_move_test --schedule_scale 1.5`). Six cases do not
     separate these; the capped option is preferred because it needs no rule change. Across the five E-1fps
     whole-move variants with the guard and box, the results were 4, 1, 3, 5 and 4 of 6.
   - The same results come out of goal_planner itself, loaded from the exported bundle (`robot_bundle_efps1`): 6/6
     pick, 4/6 whole move at full step, 5/6 whole move in-rule. A dry-run session (server over HTTP +
     `one_move_test --dry-run`) ran end to end with these flags.
3. **The paper protocol fails at fps 1** (B: 0/6 pick on every model, 0/6 whole move on every fps-1 model). It
   closes at the hover (4–5/6) or plunges 15–38 mm below the grasp height in one step. Continuous CEM with per-stage
   goals (P) is also 0/6.
4. **What did not matter or did not help.**
   - **The prior temperature** ("not too uniform"): at H=1 and H=2 every code is scored, so there is no prior. At
     H=3, t = 0 / 0.5 / 1 all fail alike. What mattered is the discrete support, not the shape of the distribution.
   - **Horizon > 1 at fps 1** fails for all three inits. H=2 descends −16 to −19 mm per step and closes on 11–28% of
     the grasp rows; H=3 does not descend at all (+0.3 mm). The goal is 1–2 steps away and the energy scores only the
     last imagined frame, so waiting and acting tie. At fps 4 (goals 5–8 steps away) H=3 is fine.
   - **The JEPA-WMs corrected init** (J-1fps, no state token): validation 0.609 vs 0.600 for the official init on the
     same data. Open loop it closes on 0.61 of grasp rows vs 0.83, and closed loop it is no better without aids. It
     does not help here.
   - **A codebook limited to the 36 mm cap** (`--limit_codes_to_cap`): 0/6 at fps 1; the planner moves sideways at
     the hover. Planning with every code and scaling the executed step (the default) is 6/6.
   - **More data helps**: E-1fps (all expert moves) beats A-1fps (first moves only) closed loop: pick 6/6 vs 3/6,
     whole move 4/6 vs 1/6.
5. **fps 4 (A-firstmove) with categorical planning** is much better open loop (gripper 0.94, lateral 0.5 mm, vs
   0.72 / 14.6 mm for the best continuous setting). In the closed-loop replay, though, it drifts sideways at the hover
   (±16.7 mm steps) in half the cases: 3/6 pick with the guard, 4/6 with hold_xy.
6. **fps 2** (E-2fps: all expert moves at 0.5 s steps, 90% of training steps under 36 mm) does not help. It is fine
   open loop but closed loop 0/6 pick: it steps sideways at the hover instead of descending (§5).

## 3. Closed loop on recorded frames (`replay/*.json`, `tools/descent_replay.py`, `tools/replay_report.py`)

Open-loop rows always start from the expert's state, so they do not show what happens after the planner's own step.
`descent_replay.py` runs the arm's loop from the hover above the source peg (`one_move_test --start hover`):
- goal frames and time schedule as the arm server sets them (the first training demo of the same transition; medians
  counted from the hover);
- plan one step, execute it, replan. "Execute" moves the pose by the planned step, clamped into goal_planner's
  workspace box, and scaled to the step cap where stated. The gripper becomes closed or open as the arm's
  gripper_intent;
- the next image is the held-out demo's recorded frame (30 Hz) nearest the new tool position with the same gripper
  state.

This is an optimistic proxy: it can only show frames the expert visited. "Pick" runs to a 30 mm lift; "whole move"
(`--until release`) continues through transit and insert to the release. A case stops when:
- **guard**: a step more than 3 mm sideways with an end below 166 mm (robot_io aborts the run there);
- **off**: no recorded frame within 15 mm (the planner left the expert's path, e.g. sideways at the hover or past the
  target);
- **early**: closed more than 15 mm above the grasp height;
- **drop / reopen**: opened after / without a grasp; in the whole move, opened more than 10 mm (5 mm laterally) from
  the demo's release pose;
- **time**: not done within 2× the expert's steps (3× for the schedule ×1.5 / ×2 rows).

Success: closed within 10 mm of the grasp height and 5 mm laterally, then lifted 30 mm closed (pick), or then opened
within 10 mm / 5 mm of the release pose (whole move). Near the target the nearest recorded frame is often 5–10 mm away
from the planned pose, so the whole-move release tolerance is at the edge of what the replay resolves.

Notes:
- The variant is `stages` (subgoals stages, no task aid). Horizon stays at the default 1: H=1 scores the 51 codes in
  one predictor batch (0.2 s per step on the H200, plus encoding the frame).
- Set `--guard_floor_mm` to the robot's low floor, i.e. robot_io's `low_floor` (workspace transit_z − 25 mm). That is
  166.13 mm per robot_io's comment of 2026-10-13, matching the recorded 191 mm transit height; the July copy of
  tower_hanoi on the cluster gives 175.32 mm. one_move_test and arm_goal_client now check this before any motion:
  they read the arm's floor from tower_hanoi's workspace.json and refuse a server whose `--guard_floor_mm` differs by
  more than 1 mm, printing the value to use. The arm's guard stays on either way; the flag only stops the planner
  from proposing steps the guard would abort.
- The schedule is time-based (the paper protocol's). If a run stalls or rushes on the arm, `--schedule_scale` is the
  knob. ×1.5 was best offline with 36 mm steps; ×2 was worse.

What the offline numbers do not cover:
- **Release tolerance.** "Whole move" success means opened within 10 mm (5 mm laterally) of the demo's release pose.
  The failed releases were 6–9 mm sideways or 7–8 mm high, about the replay's frame resolution near the target; it is
  unknown whether a ring goes onto the rod from there.
- **Leaving the recorded path.** The replay cannot show frames the expert never visited, so an "off" case means
  unknown, not failed (on the arm it may recover, or not).
- **Plumbing only in the dry run.** The dry-run session checked the client/server plumbing, not behaviour: its
  camera replays the demo whatever the arm does.
- **The approach.** Every run starts at the hover above the source peg (`--start hover`), so the approach from the
  start pose was never tested.

## 5. fps 2

E-2fps (`A_expert6all_2fps_vitg_finetune_v1`): E-1fps's setup at data.fps 2 (0.5 s steps), 15 epochs (shortened to
fit the 10 h budget), best epoch 15, val 0.593. Validation losses at different fps are not comparable. At fps 2, 47
of the 51 codes are at most 36 mm (90% of the training steps), so it would fit the arm's step rule with little scaling.

- **Open loop it looks as good as E-1fps** (cat_stages_h1): gripper 0.93, lateral 0.9 mm, vertical 1.2 mm, cosine
  1.00, close at bottom 1.00, no hover or early close. It descends at half the expert's speed (−14.6 vs −27.3 mm per
  step).
- **Closed loop it fails.**
  - Pick: 0/6 in every H=1 setting. At the hover it plans ±30 mm sideways steps (or +44 mm up) instead of descending,
    and leaves the recorded path.
  - With hold_xy it closes at the hover instead (6/6 "early").
  - Only `cat_stages_guard_h2` reaches 2/6.
  - Whole move: 2/6 with guard + box, 1/6 with the 36 mm cap.
- Same pattern as fps 4 and the 36 mm-limited codebook at fps 1: when the descent codes are short, the model ranks a
  sideways step at the hover above a descent. Only E-1fps, whose descent codes are 70–80 mm, descends from the hover.
  That matches your hunch that a large frameskip is the way to go.
- So fps 2 is not the in-rule route. E-1fps with the 36 mm execution cap (§4 option 1) is.
