# Categorical planning and fps-1 models: a clean descend + grasp offline (cluster H200, no robot), 2026-10-15

Request (2026-10-15): "make the 1 fps version work, or retrain multiple versions"; "you have 10 hr to test multiple
variants... make frameskip large (1 fps) and do something special for sampling, maybe categorical sampling? don't
sample from too uniform distribution". Fairness to the official recipe is not required for this version; every change
is still an opt-in flag (defaults unchanged) and labelled below. Nothing here touched the robot.

**Labels.** B = paper protocol (`--protocol paper`, 3 goals, CEM 800x10, H=1, the repo's box). P = planner deviation
(per-stage goals `stages`, horizon, `--sampling cat|data`, a model step other than fps 4). A = task aid (`hold_xy`,
`hold_grasp`, `hold_open`, `fix_x`). **S** = safety-envelope constraint: `--guard_floor_mm 166.13` makes the
categorical planner skip steps that robot_io's low-lateral guard would abort (more than 3 mm sideways with an end
below the low floor, transit z - 25 mm = 166.13 mm). It hardcodes that one height of the arm's workspace; it carries
no stage or goal information.

## Summary

1. **Categorical sampling is the fix.** The planner scores a finite set of actions taken from the training data:
   stay, close, open and 48 k-means centres of the expert's moving steps at the model's step
   (`--sampling cat`, `mpc_utils.cem_categorical`; every code is scored at H=1 and H=2). Every planned step is then an
   action shape the world model was trained on. Continuous CEM at fps 1 samples in a 187.5 mm box, and about 98% of
   its first samples are larger than any training step.
2. **fps 1 works with it, and the best fps-1 model is the one trained on all expert moves** (E-1fps: official init +
   state token, data.fps 1, all expert moves of the six tasks, 25 epochs; best epoch 25, val 0.588).
   - Open loop (every row of the six held-out first moves): gripper intent 0.97, lateral error 2.0 mm, vertical
     2.4 mm, direction cosine 1.00. It plans "close" on 18/18 grasp-stage rows, never at the hover or in the air.
   - Closed loop on recorded frames (§3), from the hover to a 30 mm lift: **6/6** with `cat + stages + H=1 + guard`
     (S, no task aid). Every case is: −78 mm, −23 mm, close, hold, lift +81 mm.
   - The same with each executed step capped at 36 mm (the arm's current speed rule, `--max_speed 0.036`): **6/6**.
     So fps 1 runs on the arm without changing the move-time rule.
3. **The paper protocol fails at fps 1 (B: 0/6 closed loop on every model).** It closes at the hover (4–5/6) or
   plunges 15–38 mm below the grasp height in one step. Continuous CEM with per-stage goals (P) is also 0/6.
4. **What did not matter or did not help.**
   - The prior temperature ("not too uniform"): at H=1 and H=2 every code is scored, so there is no prior. At H=3,
     t = 0 / 0.5 / 1 all fail alike. What mattered is the discrete support, not the shape of the distribution.
   - Horizon > 1 at fps 1 fails for all three inits: H=2 descends −16 to −19 mm per step and closes on 11–28% of the
     grasp rows; H=3 does not descend at all (+0.3 mm). The goal is 1–2 steps away, and the energy scores only the
     last imagined frame, so waiting and acting tie. At fps 4 (goals 5–8 steps away) H=3 is fine.
   - The JEPA-WMs corrected init (J-1fps, no state token): validation 0.609 vs 0.600 for the official init on the same
     data; open loop closes on 0.61 of grasp rows vs 0.83; closed loop no better without aids. It does not help here.
   - A codebook limited to the 36 mm cap (`--limit_codes_to_cap`): 0/6 closed loop at fps 1 (the planner moves sideways
     at the hover). Planning with every code and scaling the executed step (the default) is 6/6.
5. **fps 4 (A-firstmove) with categorical planning** is also much better open loop (gripper 0.94, lateral 0.5 mm, vs
   0.72 / 14.6 mm for the best continuous setting), but in the closed-loop replay it drifts sideways at the hover
   (±16.7 mm steps) in half the cases: 3/6 with the guard, 4/6 with hold_xy.
6. **fps 2** (E-2fps, all expert moves at 0.5 s steps, 90% of training steps under 36 mm) is training; see §5.

## 3. Closed loop on recorded frames (`replay/*.json`, `tools/descent_replay.py`, `tools/replay_report.py`)

Open-loop rows always start from the expert's state, so they do not show what happens after the planner's own step.
`descent_replay.py` runs the arm's loop from the hover above the source peg (`one_move_test --start hover`): goal frames
and time schedule as the arm server sets them (first training demo of the same transition, medians counted from the
hover), plan one step, execute it, replan. "Execute" moves the pose by the planned step, clamped into goal_planner's
workspace box (and scaled to the step cap where stated). The gripper becomes closed/open as the arm's gripper_intent.
The next image is the held-out demo's recorded frame (30 Hz, any row of the approach..transit) nearest the new tool
position with the same gripper state.

This is an optimistic proxy: it can only show frames the expert visited, and it covers the hover to a 30 mm lift
(descend, grasp, lift), not transit, insert or release. A case stops when:
- **guard**: a step more than 3 mm sideways with an end below 166 mm (robot_io aborts the run there);
- **off**: no recorded frame within 15 mm (the planner left the expert's path, e.g. sideways at the hover);
- **early**: closed more than 15 mm above the grasp height;
- **drop / reopen**: opened after / without a grasp;
- **time**: no lift by twice the expert's steps.

Success = closed within 10 mm of the grasp height and 5 mm laterally, then lifted 30 mm closed.

`cat_stages_guard_h2` P; `*_guard_*` S (+ P); `*_cap36_*` = every code planned, each executed step capped at 36 mm
(goal_planner `--max_speed 0.036` at fps 1); `*_fixx_*`, `aid_*_xy_*` (hold_xy + hold_grasp), `aid_*_xxy_*`

- **E-1fps + cat + stages + H1 + guard (S, no task aid) is 6/6, and 6/6 with the arm's 36 mm step cap.** Without the
  guard it is 3/6. The failures are a +16.6 mm x code (x is the camera's depth axis, which the model sees poorly) or a
  −8.7 mm y code at the bottom instead of "close", or a −60.6 mm y transit code at the bottom when the goal switches to
  "lift end". The arm would abort all of these. With the guard, the only options left low are vertical steps, stay,
  close and open, and the model then chooses correctly.
- With task aids (hold_xy + hold_grasp), J-1fps is also 6/6; A-1fps and E-1fps are 4/6.
- A-1fps sometimes rises 8 mm before closing and closes 11.6 mm above the grasp height (counted as a failure here; on
  the arm it may still catch the ring).

## 4. What to run on the arm (the user runs it)

The bundle for E-1fps is exported (`tools/export.sbatch`, CPU job 19905104):
`/scratch/cw5167/checkpoints/vjepa2_ac_hanoi/robot_bundle_efps1/`. It holds weights.pt (5.3 GB, epoch 25), goals.npz
(stage frames, hover schedules counted in 1 s steps, and the 51-code fps-1 action codebook), config.yaml and
MANIFEST.json. The robot needs the pushed hanoi-ac code (goal_planner `--sampling cat --guard_floor_mm`).

Server (robot GPU):

```
python -m app.vjepa_hanoi.goal_planner --fname <bundle>/config.yaml --checkpoint <bundle>/weights.pt \
    --goal_bundle <bundle>/goals.npz --protocol paper --subgoals stages --sampling cat \
    --guard_floor_mm 166.13 --max_speed 0.036 --host 127.0.0.1 --port 8766
```

- `--max_speed 0.036` × the 1 s model step = 36 mm, so arm_goal_client / one_move_test accept the server under the
  current move-time rule. A planned −78 mm step is executed as −36 mm, and the planner replans from the new frame
  (6/6 in the replay).
- Horizon stays at the default 1. H=1 scores the 51 codes in one predictor batch (0.2 s per step on the H200,
  plus encoding the frame).
- Runner: `one_move_test --variants stages` (subgoals stages, no task aid). With `--start hover`, the server's
  stage_hover_schedule ([2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 1] steps; [2, 2, 2, 4, 2, 1] for target peg A) is used.
- Check that `--guard_floor_mm` matches the robot's low floor (robot_io: workspace transit_z − 25 mm; 166.13 mm on
  2026-10-13). The arm's guard stays on either way; the flag only stops the planner from proposing steps the guard
  would abort.
- The replay only covers the descend, grasp and lift. Transit, insert and release are untested closed loop.
